Cleanroom Wipes for Semiconductor Manufacturing

Semiconductor fabrication requires the highest standards of contamination control.

Semiconductor manufacturing is one of the most contamination-sensitive industries. With feature sizes now measured in nanometers, even a single particle can ruin an entire wafer. Cleanroom wipes play a critical role in maintaining cleanliness standards.

In a typical fab, wipes are used for cleaning wafer handling equipment, removing residues, and maintaining cleanroom surfaces. Wipes must meet strict specifications for particle generation and ionic contamination.

Buyers in ISO Class 5 applications may specify particle-count, residue, ionic-extractable, material, and edge requirements. These are process and purchasing requirements, not proof that a wipe meets them. Verify the exact model with test data and the stated method.

ESD-safe wipes are important in semiconductor environments where static discharge can damage components. Anti-static treatments prevent charge buildup during wiping.

When selecting wipes for semiconductor applications, consider: particle count per IEST-RP-CC004, ionic extractables, NVR levels, and edge seal quality.

Tags: semiconductor, cleanroom wipes, wafer fabrication

ESD Wipes Guide: Protecting Sensitive Electronics

Electrostatic discharge is a hidden threat in electronics manufacturing.

ESD is one of the most common causes of electronic component failure, costing billions annually. ESD wipes prevent static charge buildup during wiping, protecting sensitive components.

In PCB assembly lines, ESD wipes clean solder paste residues, remove flux, and wipe workstations. Anti-static properties ensure no triboelectric charges discharge into sensitive components.

ESD wipes are made from continuous filament polyester or microfiber with integrated anti-static treatment. They dissipate static electricity rather than allowing accumulation.

Key applications include electronics repair stations, semiconductor handling areas, and PCB inspection stations. Use with other ESD measures like wrist straps and grounding mats.

Tags: ESD wipes, static control, electronics manufacturing

Laser Cut vs Ultrasonic Cut vs Cold Cut Wipes

Edge processing may affect wipe performance, but particle-release claims require model-specific testing.

ISO 14644 Cleanroom Classification Guide

Understand cleanroom classifications from Class 1 to Class 9.

ISO 14644-1 defines cleanroom classes based on airborne particle concentration. ISO Class 1 is cleanest, Class 9 least strict. These are room-air classifications, not grades or certificates for wiping materials.

ISO Class 5 permits no more than 3,520 particles at 0.5 microns per cubic meter. Used for semiconductor fabs and aseptic filling.

ISO Class 8 permits up to 3,520,000 particles. Suitable for general cleanroom manufacturing.

Cleanroom Wiping Procedures and Best Practices

Even the best wipes are ineffective without proper technique.

Proper wiping technique is as important as wipe quality. Move contaminants away from surfaces, not spread them.

Wipe in a single direction using the S-fold technique. Change the fold after each pass. Overlap strokes by 10%. Change wipes frequently.

For equipment cleaning, pre-wet with IPA or DI water. Use minimal pressure to avoid generating particles from the surface.

Document procedures in SOPs specifying wipe type, cleaning agent, technique, frequency, and validation.

Tags: wiping procedure, cleanroom cleaning, best practices

Non-Volatile Residue in Cleanroom Wipes

NVR measures the chemical cleanliness of cleanroom wipes.

NVR refers to solid material left after solvent evaporates from a wipe. High NVR leaves residues causing defects in semiconductor and medical device manufacturing.

Testing involves extracting wipes with solvent, evaporating, and weighing residue. Results in mg per gram of material or per square meter.

Low NVR requires careful raw material selection, controlled manufacturing, and proper packaging.
